💡 What You Learn: Tokenization & Vocabulary

Before an LLM processes meaning, text undergoes tokenization. Common words become individual tokens with fixed numeric IDs. If a word isn't in the vocabulary (like "zebra" or novel slang), subword algorithms (like Byte-Pair Encoding or WordPiece) break it down into familiar stems and letter chunks (like ##e, ##b, ##r, ##a). This ensures the model can process any word without crashing!

💡 What You Learn: Vector Embeddings & High-Dimensional Space

Real LLMs use embeddings with thousands of dimensions (e.g. 1536 or 4096 dimensions). Here we project them into 3D. Similar sentences (e.g. about dogs playing or neural networks) have vectors pointing in nearly the same direction, giving them high cosine similarity (>0.80). Different concepts point into opposite quadrants of space!

💡 What You Learn: Vector Search & RAG

This is the fundamental mechanism behind Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and semantic search:

  • When you ask ChatGPT or Claude a question, your prompt is embedded into a vector.
  • A vector database compares your query vector to millions of document vectors using distance/similarity formulas.
  • The closest matching chunks are retrieved and provided as factual context to the model!
